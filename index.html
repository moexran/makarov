They write his name in the same paragraphs as words like threat and architect. He inherited a cause built on old grievances and made it his own — cold, patient, allergic to sentiment in every room except this one.

The reports call him a symbol before they call him a man. I understand why. I also know it isn't the whole file.

What he believes about his country is his to carry. What he does for it is not something I write love letters about. But the man who takes his boots off at our door is not the man in these reports — or he is, and he simply decided the war stops at the threshold.

Konni-trained. Ghost-quiet. The kind of commander whose name alone ends arguments in rooms he isn't even in. People who've served under him describe him in two words, always the same two: controlled, and terrifying.

He plans the way other men breathe — automatically, and three moves ahead. I've watched him rearrange an entire evening because one variable shifted, the same face he'd wear rerouting a convoy.

He does not raise his voice. He has never needed to. ████████████████████████████████████████████

This is the file the reports don't have. He drinks his tea too hot on purpose. He reads my letters twice — once fast, to know I'm safe, once slow, to hear my voice in it. He is, quietly and without any apparent irony, soft with me in a way that would ruin his reputation if anyone ever saw it.

People ask how a rabbit ends up married to a wolf. I never have a clever answer. Mostly I just say: he was gentle before he was anything else to me, and everyone else only ever got to meet him after.

Case status, as far as I'm concerned: ████████████████████
